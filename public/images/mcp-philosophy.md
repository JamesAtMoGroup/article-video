# Neon Topology

A philosophy of invisible infrastructure made luminous. The subject is the hidden — protocols, signals, handshakes — things that move between machines without ever being seen. The work makes this invisible architecture glow.

Space is dark and absolute. Black is not empty but dense — a field of potential where structure emerges only where meaning demands it. Every element is earned. Nothing decorates. Every node, every line exists because the system requires it.

Form follows protocol. Nodes are precise geometric anchors — circles of uniform radius, positioned not for aesthetic pleasure but for systemic truth. Connections are deliberate paths, thin and luminous, carrying the weight of information transfer. The composition is a diagram, but a diagram elevated to artifact — meticulously drafted as if by an engineer who is also a painter.

Color is signal. Neon green (#7cffb2) is reserved for live, active connections — the current moving through the wire. Yellow (#ffd166) marks endpoints and interfaces — the boundary where one system meets another. White labels are sparse, clinical, essential. The palette is the protocol: three states, three colors, nothing more.

Typography is infrastructure annotation. Space Mono only. Small. Positioned with the care of a cartographer labeling a map that must be read under pressure. Text does not explain — it identifies. The diagram speaks; the label confirms.

The result should appear as though a senior systems architect spent a week rendering it by hand — every alignment checked, every weight deliberate, every glow radius calibrated. It is a scientific artifact that happens to be beautiful.
